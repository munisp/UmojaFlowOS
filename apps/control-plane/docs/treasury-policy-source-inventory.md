# Treasury Policy Source Inventory

## Initial primary-source findings

The proposed UmojaFlowOS treasury policy must not be mistaken for a statutory liquidity ratio or a substitute for each licensed entity’s board-approved Internal Liquidity Adequacy Assessment Process. The Central Bank of Nigeria’s risk-management guidance characterises liquidity risk as a potential loss and requires policies and strategies for individual risk elements. The Central Bank of Kenya publishes its legislation and guideline catalogue, including payment-service-provider cybersecurity guidance, which reinforces that operating controls must be anchored in the licensed entity’s regulatory perimeter. The South African Reserve Bank identifies liquidity risk among the financial risks covered by its risk-management framework. [1] [2] [3]

The recommendation phase will therefore define an **operational early-warning and approval policy**, not regulatory capital or liquidity minima. Values will be treated as configurable percentages of a corridor’s approved operating exposure or forecasted settlement need, with manual approval and source-reconciliation preconditions.
