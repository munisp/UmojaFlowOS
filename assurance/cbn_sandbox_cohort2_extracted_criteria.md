## Key extracted criteria from pages 1-5

### Programme structure
- Cohort 2 has two testing tracks:
  - **Virtual Asset Service Provider / Stablecoin / Virtual Asset Services Track (VASP Track)**
  - **Data-Enabled Financial Services Track (Non-VASP Track)**
- The sandbox is for controlled supervised testing, not automatic licensing or approval.

### Guiding principles
- Proportionality
- Technology neutrality
- Risk-based supervision
- Consumer protection
- Financial stability
- Market integrity
- Responsible innovation
- Transparency and regulatory certainty
- Regulatory coordination

### Definitions relevant to VASP assessment
- Controlled live testing under CBN supervision.
- Innovation must be new or materially different and capable of improving access, efficiency, competition, resilience, risk management, or consumer outcomes.
- Stablecoin means a digital representation of value designed to maintain stable value by reference to fiat, asset pool, or other mechanism, subject to applicable law.
- Virtual Asset Service Provider means an entity providing services relating to virtual assets, including exchange, transfer, payment, custody, wallet, issuance, or related infrastructure, subject to the applicable legal and regulatory framework.

### Objectives of Cohort 2
- Promote responsible innovation.
- Enable controlled testing of virtual asset, stablecoin, data-enabled, and related solutions.
- Improve inclusion, competition, efficiency, investment, and consumer outcomes.
- Support secure, efficient, resilient, inclusive payments and financial-services ecosystem.
- Strengthen regulatory understanding of emerging technologies.
- Reduce uncertainty through structured engagement and supervised testing.
- Provide a pathway from controlled testing to an appropriate long-term regulatory framework.

### VASP track scope
- Supports supervised testing of virtual assets and related technologies that perform, or are intended to perform, payment, settlement, or store-of-value functions, together with supporting infrastructure.
- Example eligible activities listed in the document include:
  - fiat-backed stablecoin payment and settlement solutions
  - stablecoin issuance models
  - virtual asset payment solutions
  - digital wallet infrastructure
  - virtual asset custody solutions
  - token-based payment products
  - fiat on-ramp and off-ramp services
  - exchanges using stablecoins or payment tokens
  - compliance, monitoring, and supervisory technology supporting virtual asset activities
  - payment and settlement infrastructure
- Applications involving stablecoins, custody, wallet infrastructure, token issuance, smart contracts, or other virtual-asset structures must include governance, technology, reserve management, disclosure, consumer protection, and risk-control documentation.

### Eligibility
- Invitation is based on the nature of the regulated activity and relevance to the innovation proposed, not merely possession of a licence.
- Eligible applicants may include:
  - institutions regulated by the CBN
  - VASPs and other entities developing virtual asset, stablecoin, payment, settlement, custody, wallet, tokenisation, or related financial infrastructure solutions
  - financial technology and technology companies
  - Nigerian incorporated entities developing innovations within CBN scope
  - foreign incorporated entities capable of lawful deployment in Nigeria and satisfying all applicable requirements
  - any other entity able to demonstrate that its innovation falls wholly or partly within the objectives and scope of the sandbox

### Applicant must demonstrate
- The proposed solution is sufficiently developed and ready for controlled live testing.
- The innovation addresses an identified market need or clear consumer/business/system benefit.
- Principal legal, financial, operational, technological, cybersecurity, and consumer risks have been identified and mitigated.
- Appropriate governance, consumer protection, data protection, cybersecurity, and operational resilience arrangements are in place.
- Effective AML, CFT, and CPF controls have been established where applicable.
- The applicant has operational and technical capacity to comply with sandbox reporting, monitoring, testing, and incident-notification requirements.
- The proposed innovation can be tested within clearly defined parameters without posing unacceptable risks.

### Sandbox admission and testing agreement
- Admission depends on CBN assessment of applicant, innovation, test plan, associated risks, and adequacy of safeguards.
- Successful applicants must enter into a Sandbox Testing Agreement before live testing.
- Agreement elements include:
  - approved product/service/business model/technology
  - objectives, scope, duration
  - categories and maximum number of customers/counterparties
  - transaction, exposure, geographic, or product limits
  - success criteria and performance indicators
  - reporting, monitoring, and incident-notification obligations
  - consumer protection, disclosure, safeguards, complaint handling
  - cybersecurity, data protection, operational resilience, business continuity
  - testing suspension, termination, and exit conditions

### Pre-live-testing arrangements required
- Customer identification and onboarding
- Informed customer consent and risk disclosures
- Exit rights and orderly customer treatment at test conclusion or termination
- Complaints handling and dispute resolution
- Protection of vulnerable customers where relevant
- Cybersecurity and information security
- Data protection, privacy, and customer control over personal data
- Operational resilience and business continuity
- Fraud prevention and transaction monitoring
- Incident detection, management, and reporting
- Safeguarding of customer funds and assets where applicable
- Record keeping and regulatory reporting
- Orderly scaling, suspension, transfer, or termination of the test

### Incident reporting and termination criteria
- Participants must report material incidents within 24 hours of discovery.
- CBN may vary, suspend, or terminate testing where conditions are breached, risks are not mitigated, customers/financial system are exposed to unacceptable harm, information is false/incomplete, objectives are not met, or public interest requires it.

### Evaluation and success criteria
- Achievement of agreed testing objectives
- Product or service performance
- Consumer demand, access, and outcomes
- Effectiveness of consumer-protection arrangements
- Operational resilience and cybersecurity performance
- Effectiveness of governance, compliance, and risk-management controls
- Compliance with applicable legal and regulatory requirements
- Scalability and operational sustainability
- Impact on market integrity, financial stability, monetary sovereignty, and competition
- Overall public interest

## Additional extracted criteria from pages 6-9

### Post-sandbox regulatory pathway
- Sandbox completion may lead to outcomes such as progression under an existing regulatory framework, application for licensing/approval/authorisation, further supervised development, enhanced supervisory arrangements, development or adaptation of a regulatory framework, referral or coordinated consideration with another authority, or discontinuation.
- Successful testing does **not** automatically confer a licence, approval, authorisation, registration, or entitlement to operate.
- Before test conclusion, participants must submit an exit and transition plan covering test results, outstanding risks and remediation, proposed long-term operating and regulatory model, customer/counterparty arrangements after testing, any required licence/approval/engagement, orderly scaling/suspension/transfer/termination, and any additional information required by the CBN.

### Regulatory jurisdiction and coordination
- The sandbox is for innovations within, or potentially within, the statutory mandate of the CBN.
- Where another authority has jurisdiction, the CBN may coordinate, require engagement with that authority, facilitate a post-sandbox pathway involving more than one authority, or refer the applicant to another competent authority.
- Admission does not replace any licence, registration, authorisation, approval, consent, or other statutory obligation that may be required under applicable law.
- For VASPs and other participants intersecting more than one authority, sandbox outcomes may inform a coordinated regulatory pathway, subject to applicable legal and regulatory requirements.

### Confidentiality and IP
- Commercially sensitive information submitted during application, assessment, testing, and evaluation will be treated confidentially according to applicable law and the sandbox circular.
- Participation does not transfer or otherwise affect ownership of intellectual property.

### Benefits of participation
- Controlled regulatory testing environment.
- Direct engagement with CBN supervisors.
- Regulatory feedback on products, technologies, and business models.
- Reduced uncertainty via structured engagement.
- Support for governance, risk management, consumer protection, and operational controls.
- Evidence that may support future licensing, authorisation, approval, or supervisory assessment.

### Administration and application process
- Applications are administered by the Payments System Policy Department or another designated CBN department.
- Applications must be submitted through the CBN Regulatory Sandbox Portal.
- Stated application window in the document: opening 12 August 2026; closing 31 August 2026.

### Application package requirements
- Corporate and regulatory information
- Board or management approvals
- Ownership and governance information
- Product and technology documentation
- Risk assessments and control frameworks
- Consumer protection and complaints-handling arrangements
- Cybersecurity, data protection, and business continuity documentation
- AML/CFT/CPF policies and procedures
- Testing plans, success criteria, and exit arrangements
- Proposed post-sandbox operating and regulatory model
- Evidence of financial, technical, and operational capacity
- Any mandatory annexes specified in the application questionnaire

### Application assessment criteria
- Completeness and accuracy of submission
- Eligibility of the applicant and proposed innovation
- Degree of innovation and potential market benefit
- Readiness for controlled live testing
- Alignment with Cohort 2 scope and objectives
- Adequacy of governance, risk management, compliance, and consumer protection arrangements
- Technical and operational capacity
- Feasibility of the proposed testing and exit plans
- Clarity and viability of the proposed post-sandbox regulatory pathway
- Potential impact on financial stability, monetary sovereignty, consumers, market integrity, and the payments ecosystem
- Overall suitability for testing within the CBN Regulatory Sandbox

### Immediate analytical implication for UmojaFlowOS
- The analysis must evaluate not only product controls but also **application package completeness**, organisational governance, controlled live-testing readiness, exit planning, post-sandbox pathway articulation, and cross-regulator coordination readiness.
- Even a strong codebase will still be non-ready for cohort admission if the required documentary package, sandbox test plan, board/management approvals, capacity evidence, and lawful regulatory pathway are not assembled.
