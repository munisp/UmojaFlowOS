## Executive summary

NewWave Telecom and Technologies, Inc. (“NewWave”), publicly presented as a Maryland-based technology, business-services, and data-management company, seeks consideration for participation in the Central Bank of Nigeria Regulatory Sandbox Cohort 2 VASP Track in connection with its proposed UmojaFlowOS controlled VASP-risk and evidence platform. NewWave’s public materials describe a technology business established around a “Solving for the Greater Good” mission, with capabilities spanning healthcare technology, data management and analytics, cloud migration and modernization, government-industry solutions, AI-driven capabilities, and information-security initiatives.[1][2][3]

The proposed UmojaFlowOS test is designed as a **controlled, non-custodial technology demonstration**, not as a request to operate unrestricted public exchange, custody, brokerage, token issuance, or final settlement. The proposed platform would coordinate tightly bounded test workflows, enforce transaction and participant limits, integrate AML/CFT/CPF and sanctions-screening controls, support Travel Rule data handling where applicable, apply role-based access and dual-control approvals, reconcile ledger events, preserve immutable evidence, and fail closed when required controls or external dependencies are unavailable.

The central regulatory proposition is that UmojaFlowOS can provide a controlled operating and assurance layer for VASP-related testing. The proposition remains subject to confirmation of the applying entity, Nigerian legal connection, Nigerian regulatory perimeter, CBN/SEC interface, test counterparties, customer and asset boundaries, and all applicable licensing or approval requirements. Sandbox participation must not be represented as a licence or permission to conduct activities outside the approved test perimeter.

The application is currently **not submission-ready**. The decisive open matters are corporate identity and Nigerian nexus; ownership and UBO evidence; board and authorised-signatory authority; financial capacity; existing licences and enforcement history; appointment and independence of AML/compliance officers; precise VASP category selection; quantified test limits; customer protection; AML/CFT/CPF and Travel Rule procedures; data-protection and cross-border processing; third-party due diligence; penetration testing; operational-resilience evidence; and real staging evidence for E-01 through E-09.

Subject to closure of those matters, NewWave proposes an initial test limited to approved participants, a narrowly defined geography, hard transaction and user caps, no custody or token issuance, no unapproved customer-fund exposure, continuous monitoring, immediate pause authority, incident notification, full reconciliation, evidence preservation, and an approved wind-down plan. The final test schedule, limits, KPIs, participant categories, reporting frequency, and exit criteria must be agreed with CBN and documented before entry.

NewWave must provide certified corporate records, current ownership and UBO information, financial statements, board and management evidence, and a clear Nigerian operating or partnership structure before the information can be relied on in a regulatory submission.

### Innovation and public-interest rationale

Financial-technology and virtual-asset testing requires more than a functioning transaction path. It requires a bounded product perimeter, accountable approval decisions, strong customer and financial-crime safeguards, resilient external integrations, reconciliation, incident response, and durable evidence. UmojaFlowOS is proposed to address this assurance problem by combining workflow control, identity and authority separation, screening gates, ledger reconciliation, operational monitoring, immutable evidence publication, and controlled rollback or wind-down.

The expected public-interest benefit is safer experimentation: approved innovators and regulators can observe a defined test, measure outcomes, identify control failures early, and prevent an experimental workflow from becoming an uncontrolled live financial service. Any quantitative claims regarding cost reduction, inclusion, transaction efficiency, or market impact must be supported by applicant evidence and are not asserted by this draft.

### Safeguards and test perimeter

The proposed perimeter excludes unrestricted exchange, custody, token issuance, public retail trading, and final settlement unless CBN and all other relevant Nigerian authorities expressly approve those activities. The initial test should use approved institutional or enterprise participants and, if volunteers are permitted, documented consent, clear disclosures, complaint channels, loss and refund arrangements, and a rapid pause mechanism.

The system must fail closed when identity, AML/CFT/CPF screening, Travel Rule data, ledger posting, reconciliation, approval separation, evidence integrity, or required monitoring is unavailable. A failed test must trigger controlled pause, notification, preservation of records, reconciliation, incident analysis, complaint resolution, and wind-down under an approved plan.
